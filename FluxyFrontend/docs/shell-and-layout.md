## Structure

> Moved from AGENTS.md — the core bullets stay in the index.

- **The signed-in shell is responsive, and its breakpoints are decisions rather than antd defaults.** `src/hooks/useIsCompact.ts` answers `(max-width: 991px)` through `useSyncExternalStore`, with `false` as the server snapshot so `probe:render` still measures the desktop shell. At 992px and above `AccountLayout` draws the `Sider` exactly as before. At 991px and below it draws no rail at all: the same `AccountSidebar` renders inside a left `Drawer` whose width is `min(84vw, 320px)`, titled with the area's own heading. The drawer is a layer over the page, so its open state is `AccountLayout`'s own and deliberately not persisted (the desktop rail's remains `fluxy-sidebar-collapsed`); it closes on any route change, on choosing the already-open page through `AccountSidebar`'s optional `onNavigate`, and when a resize crosses back to desktop. The header's leading button follows the mode: `MenuOutlined` with `header.openMenu` and `aria-expanded` in compact mode, the fold/unfold icons with `header.collapseMenu`/`header.expandMenu` on desktop. The account dropdown is hover-opened on desktop (a click opens it too, which is how a focused trigger answers Enter) and click-opened in compact mode, because a finger cannot hover. The logo's width lives in `src/index.css` (132px, 96px at ≤768px, hidden at ≤480px via `rootClassName`); the area title and username disappear at ≤768px; header padding is 8px and content padding 16px at ≤991px; and both the shell and the auth page carry `100dvh` behind `100vh`, so a phone measures its visible viewport rather than the one under its browser chrome.
- **Every card that owns a page toolbar carries `className="page-card"`, and the compact rules key on it.** The nineteen are `SessionsPage`, `ActiveSessionsPage`, `UsersPage`, `UserGroupsPage`, `VideoStreamsPage`, `AudioStreamsPage`, `VideoOnDemandPage`, `SeriesPage`, `SeriesEpisodesPage`, `ContentCategoriesPage`, `AdminSessionsPage`, `EpgSourcesPage`, `EpgSourceChannelsPage`, `BlacklistEntriesPage`, `BouquetsPage`, `BouquetContentPage`, `TariffPlansPage`, `SubscriptionsPage` and `ConnectionLogsPage`; the class is what keeps the rules from reaching a card with no toolbar, such as the profile cards or the session cards inside `ActiveSessionsPage`. At ≤1200px `.ant-card-head-wrapper` becomes a column, so the heading takes a row of its own and the toolbar moves under it — measured at 800/992/1024px, the title was width 0 while every control stayed visible, and the title row is the fix. At ≤768px each `.ant-space` in `extra` becomes a column, every control is full width (including the `span` that `hinted` wraps around a disabled button), the heading may wrap (the desktop pages pin `whiteSpace: nowrap` inline, which is why that one rule needs `!important`), and card head/body padding is 16px. Desktop stays one row above 1200px. `probe:render` asserts the class on every toolbar card it renders (and names the one that lacks it) and checks that both stylesheet halves exist; a page added later without the class silently keeps the broken desktop header on a phone.
- **The box must not change width on its own, and `scrollbar-gutter: stable` on `.layout-shell__content` is what guarantees it.** The fit divides the measured width, so the measurement has to be a fact about the window rather than about whether a scrollbar happens to be showing: on a table that overflows horizontally, `.ant-table-content`'s scrollbar adds height, that height crosses the viewport threshold, the content area grows a classic space-taking scrollbar, its box narrows by those pixels, the fit is recomputed narrower, the horizontal scrollbar is no longer needed, its height goes away, the vertical one goes away, the box widens - and the page repaints that forever. The activity table is where the owner saw it (its end cell is a second line, so its rows are the tallest here, and under RU the toolbar wraps besides), but the loop belongs to the measure-and-divide frame, not to that page. It does not reproduce in headless Chromium, whose scrollbars are overlays; it needs a desktop browser with classic scrollbars. **The table fits the box it is drawn in, and keeps a width of its own when the box is too small for that.** `ServerTable` measures its own box with a `ResizeObserver` — the box is the card's body, and its width is a fact about the window, the zoom and the sidebar (164 pixels of difference on its own), so a table that assumes a width overflows on somebody's screen and not on the one it was written on, and the column that disappears is the one at the far right: the actions. The room is then divided between the columns by `distributeWidths` (`src/utils/tableWidths.ts`), in the proportions the visitor arranged them in: **a column that truncates — one with `ellipsis` — may be narrower, and a column that holds something fixed — a logo, a tag, a row's buttons — keeps its width**, because its content has no smaller form. The actions column is the one this matters for: squeezing it would put the buttons outside their own cell, which is the very thing `actionsColumnWidth` measured. The division is done here rather than left to the browser because a browser stretches a fixed-layout table that is narrower than its box, and it stretches every column in proportion — which would hand the actions column back the very dead space the measurement was taken to remove. **The table has a floor**: a squeezed column stops at `MIN_COLUMN_WIDTH` (90 — the number the visitor cannot drag a column past either, for a different reason: a dragged column's floor is about the resize handle staying reachable, a squeezed one about the column keeping its name), and the table keeps the sum of those floors when the box is narrower still — it then scrolls inside the card, which is what a table on a phone is supposed to do. The room a floored column does not need goes to the columns that can still take it (without that the sum of the shares is *larger* than the room, and a table wider than its box is the one thing the whole exercise exists to prevent), and the shares are rounded by their remainders so the sum is the room exactly — a table a pixel narrower than the box is stretched by the browser, and the column that grows in that stretch is the actions one. The headings of a squeezed column **wrap** rather than being cut: antd's `ellipsis` reaches the heading as well as the cells, and a column showing a letter where a heading should be is a column with no name (`src/index.css`, whose selector is longer than it looks — antd's own rule is three classes and a shorter one loses the cascade). The visitor's own widths are untouched by all of it: the fitting is a fact about the screen, not about the arrangement.
